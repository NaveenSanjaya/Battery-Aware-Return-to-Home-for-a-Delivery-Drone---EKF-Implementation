This project set out to let a delivery drone decide for itself when to fly home, using only a noisy, biased current sensor and a voltage sensor whose readings sag under load. It combined an Extended Kalman Filter, which estimates the battery's state of charge together with its uncertainty, with a return-to-home rule that turns that uncertainty into a safety margin. Both were tested in a MATLAB simulator whose battery deliberately differs from the filter's model.

\section{Answers to the objectives}\label{sec:c_objectives}

\textbf{A realistic simulator.} The simulator produced the expected battery behaviour: a voltage sag of 367\,mV per cell at take-off, steps in the right direction at every change of flight phase, and a charge-counting drift with a biased sensor ($-2.3\%$ in 14.2\,min) that matched the theoretical value of about 2.4\%. Its true battery has a second, slow RC pair and parameters offset by 10\%, so the filter was never tested against its own model.

\textbf{An EKF that estimates the SoC and its uncertainty.} A design-stage investigation found that a wrong internal resistance was the main source of SoC error, so the filter learns $R_0$ during flight. On the nominal flight (E1) this filter (stage~B) reached an SoC RMSE of 0.97\%, against 4.36\% for charge counting and 2.51\% for the filter without $R_0$, with the truth inside its $\pm 3\sigma$ band 100\% of the time; its resistance estimate ended at 8.85\,m$\Omega$ against a true 8.8\,m$\Omega$. From a wrong start (E2: true 85\%, assumed 100\%) it was within 2\% after 42\,s (RMSE 1.32\%), while charge counting stayed wrong all flight (RMSE 13.85\%). Adding the sensor bias and the capacity as states (stage~C) could not separate those two within one flight, because only the discharge rate $(I_m - b)/C_n$ is observable, but it got that rate right. Over 20 flights per scenario, stage~C was the only filter whose band held the truth 100\% of the time in all of the nominal, wrong-start, biased-sensor and aged-battery scenarios, with mean RMSE between 1.03\% and 1.80\%. Stage~B, the most accurate on a healthy battery, overestimated the SoC of an aged battery by 8.34\% on average with only 27\% coverage. Stage~C was therefore chosen to drive the decision: the most accurate filter is not always the safest one to make decisions with.

\textbf{A risk-aware return-to-home rule.} The rule turns back when the probability of landing with the 10\% reserve falls below 95\%, using the filter's SoC uncertainty and a trip cost based on the observable drain rate. In the demonstration mission it turned back half a minute earlier than the estimate-only rule and landed with 17.8\% instead of 11.9\%, while charge counting led the drone to a battery failure 200\,m from home.

\textbf{Evaluation against baselines.} In 500 random missions with random start charge, sensor bias, wind, battery age and battery parameters, the risk-aware rule (D) had no battery failures and broke the reserve in 2.8\% of missions, inside its promise of at most 5\%, leaving 6.1\% of the battery unused on average. The same filter used without its uncertainty (C) also had no failures, but broke the reserve in 38.0\% of missions. Charge counting (B) let the battery die in 30.4\% of missions, and in 58.2\% of those with an aged battery. The voltage threshold (A) died in 9.2\% and broke the reserve in 32.2\%. Over these missions the stage-C filter had an SoC RMSE of 1.31\% against 3.35\% for charge counting, with 100\% band coverage. Across a sweep of thresholds from 80\% to 99.9\%, the share of missions that broke the reserve stayed within the promised $1 - p^{\ast}$ every time, so the probabilities were calibrated and slightly conservative. To match the risk-aware rule's safety, the voltage threshold had to rise to 3.55\,V, where it left 17.9\% of the battery unused and completed about half as many deliveries.

Together, these results show that an EKF is effective for this application when its model includes the dominant error source, that its uncertainty is as valuable as its estimate, and that a decision rule which uses an honest uncertainty can keep an explicit safety promise at a modest cost in range.

\section{Limitations and future work}\label{sec:c_next}

The results come from simulation with a representative OCV curve, no temperature effects and one route, and the method reaches its limit when the true battery differs strongly from the filter's model (Section~\ref{sec:d_limitations}). The most useful next steps are to calibrate the current-sensor bias at rest before take-off and estimate the capacity between flights, which would make both individually observable; to add a second RC pair or adaptive noise to the filter for robustness to model mismatch; to compare the EKF with a UKF and a particle filter; and to validate the estimator on real flight logs such as the CMU delivery-drone and eVTOL datasets.
