\section{Problem and approach}\label{sec:intro_problem}

A delivery drone must decide, during flight, when to stop delivering and return home. It cannot measure its remaining charge, the \emph{state of charge} (SoC), directly. It only has a noisy, biased current sensor and a voltage that dips under load.

Turning back too late risks losing the drone; turning back too early wastes battery and deliveries. A better decision than a fixed safety margin needs both an SoC estimate and a measure of how uncertain it is, so that the margin can shrink when the estimate is precise and grow when it is not.

\label{sec:intro_why_ekf}A Kalman filter provides both: it weights a model-based prediction against a measurement and reports its own uncertainty. Because a battery's resting voltage is a curved function of its SoC (Section~\ref{sec:bg_ocv}), the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) is used. It is the classic approach in battery management systems~\cite{plett2004part1,plett2004part3}; a particle filter would cost far more computation for little benefit on this smooth curve~\cite{simon2006optimal}.

\section{Objectives and contributions}\label{sec:intro_objectives}\label{sec:intro_contributions}

Using a MATLAB simulator that knows the true SoC, the project aims to:
\begin{itemize}
  \item design an EKF that estimates the SoC and its uncertainty and learns the internal resistance in flight, the main source of SoC error (Section~\ref{sec:m_ekf_investigation});
  \item choose the filter used for decisions by how honest its uncertainty is, not only by its accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:m_ekf_choice});
  \item design a return-to-home (RTH) rule that turns back when the probability of getting home with a safety reserve drops below 95\% (Section~\ref{sec:m_rth});
  \item compare both with charge counting and a voltage threshold over 500 random missions (Section~\ref{sec:r_sweep}).
\end{itemize}
