\section{Why the filter works, and what one flight can reveal}\label{sec:d_why}\label{sec:d_observability}

Charge counting has no way to notice its own mistakes, so a wrong start, a sensor bias or a smaller battery stay wrong for the whole flight. The EKF gets an independent check from the voltage every second, weighted by the Kalman gain (Section~\ref{sec:bg_gain}). That check is weakest where the OCV curve is flat, so there the gain is small and the uncertainty grows, as E7 showed (Figure~\ref{fig:r_e7}): the filter knows when it knows less, which is what the return-to-home rule needs.

Not every state is equally visible. The resistance shows whenever the current changes, because only the sag changes with the current; the end of take-off was enough to learn it. The bias and the capacity overlap with each other and with the resistance during steady cruise \eqref{eq:m_confound}, so stage~C's individual estimates were sometimes wrong, even in sign (E3, E4). The voltage does fix their combination, the drain rate \eqref{eq:m_rate}, which is all the SoC and the trip cost need; this is why the trip cost uses the rate.

\section{Model errors and tuning}\label{sec:d_mismatch}

The largest errors came from the model, not from the sensors. A 10\% resistance error is systematic, not random, so telling the filter its sensor is noisier does not remove it (Section~\ref{sec:m_ekf_investigation}). Worse, the filter cannot see the problem itself: stage~A's NIS looked healthy while its NEES was 30 to 400 times too large (Table~\ref{tab:r_e8}). Only a simulation, where the truth is known, exposes this. Learning the resistance removed most of the error, but a large enough model error (E6) defeats every version of the filter.

Tuning sets how cautious the filter is rather than how accurate (E5). The chosen settings are slightly cautious on purpose, because the decision rule turns the filter's uncertainty directly into a safety margin. A more advanced filter would not fix the main problem: with a perfect model the EKF's error was only 0.12\%, so the tangent-line approximation is not what limits accuracy. An unscented Kalman filter or a particle filter~\cite{simon2006optimal,purdue_jate_comparison} would cost more computation and inherit the same model error.

\section{The most accurate filter is not the safest}\label{sec:d_choice}\label{sec:d_value}

Stage~B was the most accurate filter on a healthy battery, yet on an aged battery it was confidently wrong in the dangerous direction: it believed there was more charge than there was (E4, E8). For a decision that depends on the uncertainty, honesty matters more than the last fraction of a percent of accuracy, so stage~C drives the return-to-home rule.

Policies C and D use the same filter and differ only in whether they use its uncertainty. Policy~C aims exactly at the reserve, so it lands below it about as often as above. Policy~D keeps a buffer that grows when the filter is unsure, and at every threshold its breach rate stayed within its promise (Table~\ref{tab:r_mc}, Figure~\ref{fig:r_tradeoff}), so the probabilities mean what they say. This tests the whole chain at once: the model, the filter's uncertainty, the trip cost and the probability. The price is modest, about 6\% of the battery unused and slightly fewer deliveries than policy~C, and the threshold lets an operator move along the trade-off. The baselines fail for clear reasons: charge counting becomes optimistic with an aged battery or a sensor that reads low, and a voltage threshold ignores the distance home and is fooled by sag.

\section{Limitations and future work}\label{sec:d_limitations}\label{sec:d_future}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Simulation only.} Public flight logs~\cite{cmu_dji_dataset,cmu_evtol_paper} lack the true SoC but could test the voltage predictions.
  \item \textbf{Representative battery data.} The OCV curve is a typical shape, not measured; temperature and hysteresis are not modelled.
  \item \textbf{Simple decision model.} Gaussian errors, a straight flight home and a constant cruise current are assumed.
  \item \textbf{Bias and capacity.} Calibrating the bias at rest before take-off and estimating the capacity between flights would separate them.
  \item \textbf{Model error.} A two-RC filter model or adaptive noise settings could reduce the errors under strong mismatch (E6).
\end{itemize}
