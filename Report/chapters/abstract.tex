A delivery drone must decide during flight when to stop delivering and fly home, but it cannot measure how much charge is left in its battery. Its current sensor is noisy and slightly wrong, and its voltage drops whenever the motors work hard. This project uses an Extended Kalman Filter (EKF) that combines the two sensors to estimate the battery's state of charge (SoC) and how uncertain that estimate is. A return-home rule then turns the drone back when it is less than 95\% sure of landing with 10\% charge left.

Everything is tested in a MATLAB simulation whose battery is deliberately different from the filter's model, so the test is fair. Early tests showed that the battery's internal resistance caused most of the SoC error, so the filter learns the resistance during flight. The final filter also estimates the current-sensor error and the battery capacity. Over 500 random missions its SoC error was 1.31\%, against 3.35\% for simple charge counting, and its uncertainty stayed honest in every normal test, including an aged battery.

In 500 random missions, the return-home rule never let the battery run out and landed below the 10\% reserve in only 2.8\% of missions, within its 5\% promise. The same filter without its uncertainty landed below the reserve in 38\% of missions, and charge counting let the battery die in 30\%. The rule never broke its promise at any safety level tested, and was in fact about twice as safe as promised, so the filter's uncertainty can be trusted for safety decisions.
